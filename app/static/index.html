The user's job, current process and cost
One decision
Agree a landing time, taxi route and gate for a disrupted arrival, then review the evidence before booking simulated resources.
Today
The challenge describes phone calls and manual software updates between ATC, flight crew and ground staff. Each team has different information; a changed plan needs everyone to recheck.
Cost and risk
Extra holding consumes fuel; a different gate can increase crew overtime or miss turnaround targets. Conflicting or stale decisions can use unavailable resources. We show these modeled quantities for each plan; manual coordination time and real monetary savings have not been measured.
Prototype boundary
One arrival, a declared runway/gate subset and simulated current conditions. The reviewer represents a coordination lead; real deployment would retain each operational authority's decisions.

2 Three agents negotiate

Up to 3 turns · 45-second limit

Who decides what · rules, agents and human authority
Python rules
Enumerate landing, gate and taxi options; calculate fuel, timing and cost; check runway spacing, capacity, gate, crew, equipment and turnaround. Failed or unknown hard checks block review. Fixed rules also choose the fallback.
Three LLM agents
ATC, Pilot and Ground receive separate requests, express preferences and explain concessions using supplied evidence. Each sends one structured message per turn. Agreement requires three accepts of the identical plan; agents cannot change rules or authorize bookings.
You
Review the checks and baseline comparison, then approve or reject with a reason. Approval rechecks current state and atomically books the runway, gate, crew and equipment in the simulator.
